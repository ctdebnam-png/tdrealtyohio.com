Buying or selling a home in Columbus

Columbus is Ohio's capital, founded in 1812 where the Scioto and Olentangy rivers meet. It is often called a city of neighborhoods, and the names people search for are usually neighborhood names: Clintonville, German Village, the Short North, Victorian Village, Franklinton and many more.

Talk With Travis About Columbus

Columbus at a glance

Counties
Franklin, the county seat, with parts of the city in Delaware and Fairfield
Public schools
Columbus City Schools, the largest district in Ohio; parts of the city are in the Dublin, Hilliard, New Albany-Plain, South-Western, Westerville and Worthington districts
Downtown
The Scioto Mile riverfront parks, Columbus Commons and the Arena District
Universities
The Ohio State University and Columbus State Community College
Parks
Metro Parks, the Columbus and Franklin County Metropolitan Park District
Headquarters in the area
Nationwide, American Electric Power, Huntington Bancshares and Vertiv, with Cardinal Health in Dublin

Distances are measured in a straight line from the Ohio Statehouse to the city's listed coordinates; driving distance is longer. Confirm the city, township and school district for any specific address on the county auditor's record.

Around Columbus

Downtown has been the focus of recent revitalization: the Columbus Commons park, the parks of the Scioto Mile along a reshaped riverfront, and new development in the Arena District and Franklinton. North Market, a public market and food hall, sits downtown near the Short North, where the city rebuilt its historic metal arches over High Street in 2002. Across the county, Metro Parks runs regional parks and trails in and around the city.

Selling in Columbus

Columbus is large enough that a citywide view of the market tells a seller very little. Pricing starts with the homes that compete with yours: the same neighborhood, the same school district, and a similar age and style of house. Travis builds the list price from those sales and from what is active nearby, then sets prep and launch around what buyers for that kind of home ask about first.

Some older neighborhoods carry local rules. The city maintains historic districts in German Village, Italian Village, Victorian Village and the Brewery District, and exterior work there can need approval. If you have replaced windows, roofing or siding, gather the approvals and permits before listing. Ohio's residential property disclosure form, and the federal lead-based paint disclosure for homes built before 1978, apply across the city.

Buying in Columbus

A Columbus address does not tell you which schools serve it. Portions of the city fall in at least six suburban districts as well as Columbus City Schools, and the line can run between neighboring streets. Travis checks the Franklin County Auditor record for every home you consider, so the district, the taxing jurisdiction and any special assessments are known before you make an offer.

Buyers in Columbus are usually choosing between very different kinds of housing: older homes in the neighborhoods near downtown, postwar houses further out, and newer construction at the edges of the city. Travis helps you compare them on the things that drive the decision, such as commute, condition, lot and long-term upkeep, then handles the offer, inspection, appraisal and closing.
