Buying or selling a home in Upper Arlington

Upper Arlington was developed by brothers King and Ben Thompson, whose Upper Arlington Company was incorporated in 1917. The southern half of the city, Old Arlington, has been on the National Register of Historic Places since 1985, and the main campus of The Ohio State University is a short distance east.

Around Upper Arlington

Upper Arlington's parks and public events are a large part of the city's identity. The Labor Day Arts Festival at Northam Park draws more than 25,000 visitors and about 200 artist vendors. A free summer concert series plays at the Amelita Mirolo Barn in Sunny 95 Park, and the Upper Arlington Civic Association's Fourth of July celebration brings a parade along Northwest Boulevard and an evening festival. The city runs three outdoor pools: Tremont Pool, Devon Pool and the Reed Road Water Park. The Miller Park library branch occupies the Upper Arlington Company's original field office.

Selling in Upper Arlington

Upper Arlington's housing was built in two broad waves: the original section south of Lane Avenue from the 1910s onward, and the postwar neighborhoods north of it. The two sell differently. A home in Old Arlington is compared with other homes of its era and condition, and its updates are the story. A home further north is compared with its own neighborhood. Travis sets the price from the right set of sales, not a citywide average.

National Register listing places no federal restrictions on a private owner, but buyers still ask what it means, and the answer should be accurate. For older homes, gather records of roof, electrical, plumbing and sewer work, and remember the federal lead-based paint disclosure for homes built before 1978. Buying in Upper Arlington

One school district serves the whole city, which makes the district question simpler here than in many suburbs. The questions that remain are about the house: its age, what has been updated, and what will need work soon. Travis tours with you and helps you read each home's history before you decide what to offer.

Older homes reward a careful inspection, including a camera inspection of the sewer line. Travis helps you decide what to ask the seller to repair or credit and what to accept, sets your offer from comparable sales nearby, and manages appraisal, financing deadlines and closing.
