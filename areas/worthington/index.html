Buying or selling a home in Worthington

Worthington was founded in 1803 by the Scioto Company, led by James Kilbourne, and named for Thomas Worthington, later governor of Ohio. Its downtown still centers on the Village Green at the crossing of State Route 161 and U.S. Route 23.

Around Worthington

Worthington was settled by the Scioto Company, organized in Granby, Connecticut, which bought 16,000 acres along the Whetstone River, now the Olentangy. The Orange Johnson House was built north of the Village Green in the town's first years, and General William Henry Harrison issued orders from Worthington in 1812. Columbus's two commercial airports, John Glenn Columbus International and Rickenbacker International, serve Worthington, and the Ohio State University Airport is the nearest general aviation field.

Selling in Worthington

Worthington's housing runs from homes near the Village Green, where the city's earliest buildings stand, to postwar neighborhoods and later subdivisions. The age of the house shapes the sale. Older homes bring questions about original systems, past additions and, for anything built before 1978, the federal lead-based paint disclosure. Travis goes through those with you before launch so they are answered in the disclosure, not discovered in the inspection.

Ohio requires most sellers to give the buyer a completed residential property disclosure form before accepting an offer. In an older home, that form is where records of past work matter: roof dates, electrical updates, sewer line work and permits. Gather what you have. TD Realty Ohio then sets the price from nearby sales of similar homes, plans prep and launch, and manages offers through closing.

Buying in Worthington

The Worthington Schools boundary is larger than the city, and parts of Columbus are in the district. That widens the search if the district is part of your criteria, and it means a Worthington address and a Worthington Schools parcel are not always the same thing. Travis checks the Franklin County Auditor record for each home so you know the city, the district and the taxing jurisdiction.

If you are looking at an older home, plan for a thorough inspection and ask about a camera inspection of the sewer line. Travis helps you decide which findings to raise with the seller and which to accept, prices your offer from comparable sales, and keeps the appraisal, financing and closing on schedule.
