FSBO vs. Realtor in Columbus: Which Option Actually Saves You More?

TD Realty Ohio | February 2026

Every Columbus homeowner selling their property faces the same tempting thought: "Why pay an agent thousands in commission to sell a home?" It is a reasonable question. On a $350,000 home, a traditional 3% listing commission comes to $10,500. That is real money, and the idea of keeping it sounds great on paper.

But here is what the FSBO (For Sale By Owner) pitch leaves out: commission savings are only one side of the equation. The other side includes the price you actually get, the time it takes to sell, the legal risks you carry, and the hidden costs you did not see coming. When you factor everything in, FSBO often costs sellers more than it saves them.

This article comes from TD Realty Ohio, a brokerage that offers 1-2% listing commissions. In the interest of transparency: there is an obvious perspective here. But sellers deserve an honest breakdown of all three options: FSBO, traditional agent, and low-commission agent, so they can make the best decision for their situation. Some sellers absolutely should go FSBO. Most should not. Here is how to figure out which camp you fall into.

What Selling FSBO Actually Involves

Many sellers underestimate the sheer volume of work involved in selling a home. When you hire an agent, dozens of tasks happen behind the scenes that you never think about. Go FSBO, and every single one of them lands on your plate.

Pricing Your Home Correctly

This is where most FSBO sellers make their first and most expensive mistake. Without access to the Columbus REALTORS MLS and the ability to run a proper comparative market analysis, you are essentially guessing. Zillow's Zestimate and similar tools can be off by 5-10% or more, and in a market where neighborhoods like Clintonville, Westerville, and Columbus proper can vary dramatically block by block, pricing accuracy matters enormously.

Price too high and your home sits on the market, accumulating days that make buyers suspicious. Price too low and you leave money on the table. Either way, you lose.

Photography and Marketing

Buyers today start their search online, and the first thing they see is photos. Professional real estate photography, with proper lighting, wide-angle lenses, and editing, makes a measurable difference in how many showings you get. FSBO sellers who use their phone camera are competing against professionally photographed listings, and the comparison is not kind.

Beyond photos, you need a compelling listing description, syndication to major platforms (Zillow, Realtor.com, Redfin), social media marketing, and potentially virtual tours. An MLS listing handles much of the syndication automatically, but FSBO sellers without MLS access have to manually list on each platform.

Getting on the MLS

The MLS is where roughly 90% of home sales originate. Buyer agents search the MLS to find homes for their clients, and the MLS feeds data to every major real estate website. Without it, your home is essentially invisible to most of the buyer pool.

FSBO sellers in Ohio cannot access the MLS directly. You need a licensed broker to submit the listing. Flat-fee MLS services exist (more on costs below), but the listing is often bare-bones, you may have limited ability to update it, and your home will be flagged as FSBO, which some buyer agents treat differently.

Showings and Open Houses

Every showing requires you to be available, or to install a lockbox and trust strangers in your home unsupervised. You need to coordinate schedules with prospective buyers (and their agents), keep your home show-ready at all times, and handle the feedback loop after each visit. For most working professionals, managing showings alone can become a part-time job.

Negotiations

This is where FSBO sellers are at the greatest disadvantage. You will be negotiating directly with either a buyer's agent (who does this for a living) or a buyer who may have their own agenda. Offer terms, inspection contingencies, appraisal gaps, closing credits, earnest money, possession dates: each of these is a negotiation point, and experienced agents know how to navigate them to protect their client's interests.

When a buyer's agent submits an offer with a $15,000 inspection credit request, do you know if that is reasonable or a lowball tactic? When a buyer asks for seller-paid closing costs, do you understand how that affects your net proceeds compared to a lower purchase price? These decisions involve thousands of dollars, and negotiation experience matters.

Paperwork and Legal Compliance

Ohio real estate transactions involve a substantial amount of paperwork: the purchase agreement, seller disclosures, lead paint disclosures, title work, closing documents, and more. Missing a deadline or filling out a form incorrectly can delay your closing or expose you to legal liability. We will cover Ohio-specific requirements in detail below.

One of the biggest misconceptions about FSBO is that it is "free." It is not. Here is what you will likely spend even without a listing agent:

Wait, $10,000 to $13,000? That does not sound like much savings compared to hiring an agent. And it gets worse when you look at the data on sale prices.

FSBO Sale Price Data: The Number That Changes Everything

Here is the statistic that should give every potential FSBO seller pause: according to the National Association of REALTORS 2024 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers, FSBO homes sold for a median price of $310,000, compared to $405,000 for agent-assisted homes. That is a 23% gap.

Now, some of that difference is explained by the types of properties sold FSBO (more rural, lower-value, or sold to known buyers at below-market prices). The gap is not entirely apples-to-apples. But even conservative estimates suggest FSBO sellers typically net 5-10% less than they would with professional representation, because of pricing mistakes, weaker negotiation, and limited market exposure.

Let us put that in Columbus terms. The median home price in the Columbus metro area is approximately $340,000-$360,000. Let us use $350,000 for our math:

  • If FSBO sells for 10% less: $315,000 sale price. You "saved" $7,000-$10,500 in listing commission but lost $35,000 in sale price. Net loss: $24,500 - $28,000.
  • If FSBO sells for 5% less: $332,500 sale price. You "saved" $7,000-$10,500 but lost $17,500. Net loss: $7,000 - $10,500.
  • Even at just 3% less: $339,500 sale price. You lost $10,500, which is roughly what a traditional 3% commission would have cost anyway.

The math is clear: if selling FSBO results in even a slightly lower sale price, you have wiped out any commission savings and then some. And you did all the work yourself.

Ohio-Specific Legal Requirements for Sellers

Selling a home in Ohio comes with specific legal obligations that apply whether you use an agent or not. Miss any of these, and you could face delays, lawsuits, or financial penalties.

Residential Property Disclosure Form

Ohio Revised Code Section 5302.30 requires sellers to complete a Residential Property Disclosure Form. This document covers the condition of your home's structure, roof, plumbing, electrical, HVAC, water and sewer systems, environmental hazards, and more. You must disclose any known material defects. Failure to disclose known issues can result in the buyer suing you after closing, even years later.

This is not a form you want to fill out without guidance. An incorrect or incomplete disclosure is one of the most common sources of post-sale litigation in Ohio.

Lead-Based Paint Disclosure

If your Columbus home was built before 1978, federal law requires you to provide buyers with a Lead-Based Paint Disclosure form, a copy of the EPA pamphlet "Protect Your Family From Lead in Your Home," and a 10-day period to conduct a lead inspection. This is not optional, and the penalties for non-compliance are severe: up to $19,507 per violation under federal law.

Ohio Conveyance Fee (Transfer Tax)

Ohio charges a conveyance fee when real property changes hands. The mandatory state rate is $1 per $1,000 of the sale price. Franklin County adds a permissive fee on top of that. On a $350,000 home in Columbus, expect to pay roughly $350 at the state level plus the county portion. Your title company handles this at closing, but you need to budget for it.

Agency Disclosure

Ohio law requires an Agency Disclosure form any time a real estate licensee is involved in a transaction. Even as a FSBO seller, if the buyer has an agent, that agent must present this disclosure. Understanding agency relationships (who represents whom and what duties they owe) is critical to protecting yourself during negotiations.

Other Considerations

Columbus and Franklin County may have additional requirements around zoning compliance, point-of-sale inspections (depending on the municipality), and HOA disclosure packages. Municipalities like Westerville, Dublin, and Reynoldsburg each have their own rules. If you are selling FSBO, researching the specific requirements for your municipality is entirely your responsibility.

When FSBO Might Actually Make Sense

To be fair, there are situations where selling FSBO is a perfectly reasonable choice:

Selling to a Family Member or Friend

If you already have a buyer and the price is agreed upon, you do not need an agent to find one. You will still want a real estate attorney to handle the paperwork ($500-$1,000), but you can skip the marketing, showings, and negotiation. This is the scenario where FSBO makes the most financial sense.

You Have an Investor Buyer Lined Up

Cash investors who buy "as-is" properties often approach homeowners directly. If you have a legitimate offer from a reputable investor, the transaction is usually simpler and an attorney can handle the closing. Just make sure the offer price is fair; investors are professional negotiators who buy below market value.

You Have Real Estate Experience

If you are a licensed agent, a real estate attorney, or have successfully sold multiple properties before, you have the knowledge to handle the process. You understand pricing, contracts, disclosures, and negotiation. For experienced sellers, FSBO can be a smart move.

Your Market Is Extremely Hot

In a strong seller's market with extremely low inventory, homes sometimes sell quickly regardless of how they are marketed. If Columbus is experiencing a severe housing shortage in your price range and neighborhood, buyers may come to you. But even in hot markets, proper pricing and negotiation can mean the difference between a good price and a great one.

The Property Is Low Value or Unusual

For vacant land, very low-value properties, or situations where the commission would represent a disproportionate share of the sale price, FSBO may make practical sense. The cost-benefit of agent representation is strongest for typical residential properties in the $200,000-$800,000 range.

The Bottom Line: Do the Full Math Before Deciding

Selling FSBO is not about whether you can do it. Of course you can. The question is whether you should, and the answer depends on your honest assessment of three things:

  1. Your likely sale price - Will you price it right? Will your marketing attract the same buyer pool? Will you negotiate as effectively as an experienced agent?
  2. Your time and stress tolerance - Managing showings, fielding calls, negotiating offers, and handling paperwork is significant work. What is your time worth?
  3. Your actual savings - After flat-fee MLS, photography, attorney fees, and the near-certainty of buyer agent compensation, the savings over a low-commission agent are minimal.

For the small percentage of sellers with a buyer already lined up or real estate experience, FSBO can work well. For everyone else, the data strongly suggests that professional representation, especially at a low commission rate, delivers better financial outcomes.
