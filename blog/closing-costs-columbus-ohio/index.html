Closing Costs When Selling a Home in Central Ohio

By Travis Debnam, Broker at TD Realty Ohio | February 2, 2026

When you sell a home in Columbus or anywhere in Central Ohio, the sale price on your purchase agreement is not the amount you walk away with. Between title fees, transfer taxes, real estate commissions, and several other line items, seller closing costs can consume a significant portion of your proceeds. Understanding these costs before you list is essential for setting realistic expectations and making informed financial decisions.

TD Realty Ohio handles transactions across Franklin, Delaware, Licking, and Fairfield counties, and sellers are often surprised by closing costs. This guide breaks down every fee you are likely to encounter, provides a real-world net proceeds example on a $350,000 Columbus home, and explains specific strategies to reduce what you pay at the closing table.

What Are Seller Closing Costs?

Closing costs are the fees and expenses that must be paid when a real estate transaction is finalized. For sellers, these costs are typically deducted from the sale proceeds at closing. The title company or closing attorney prepares a settlement statement that itemizes every charge, and the remaining balance after all deductions is your net proceeds, which is the actual money you receive.

In Ohio, sellers do not write a check at closing under normal circumstances. Instead, the title company deducts all seller-side costs from the sale proceeds before disbursing the remainder to you. This means the costs feel somewhat invisible until you see that final settlement statement, which is exactly why it is important to understand them upfront.

For most Central Ohio sellers, total closing costs (including commissions) range from 6% to 10% of the sale price. Excluding commissions, the non-negotiable fees typically fall between 2% and 4%. The specific amount depends on your home's sale price, which county you are in, and what you have negotiated with the buyer.

Real Estate Commissions

Commissions are by far the largest closing cost for most sellers. In Central Ohio, the traditional listing agent commission has historically been around 3% of the sale price, with an additional amount offered to compensate the buyer's agent. On a $350,000 home, a 3% listing commission alone is $10,500.

This is the single most controllable closing cost you have. By working with a low-commission listing agent like TD Realty Ohio, you can reduce your listing side commission to 1-2% without sacrificing the services that actually sell your home: MLS listing, professional photography, showing coordination, negotiation, and contract management.

Following recent industry changes stemming from the NAR settlement, buyer's agent compensation is now negotiated separately and more transparently. Your listing agent can help you understand the options and structure a competitive offer that attracts qualified buyers while keeping your costs manageable.

Transfer Tax (Ohio Conveyance Fee)

Ohio charges a real estate transfer tax, officially called the conveyance fee, whenever property changes hands. The fee has two components:

  • Mandatory state fee: $1 per $1,000 of the sale price (set by Ohio Revised Code 322.02)
  • County permissive tax: An additional amount set by each county, up to $3 per $1,000

In Franklin County (Columbus, Westerville, Upper Arlington, Worthington, Hilliard, Grove City, Gahanna, Reynoldsburg), the permissive tax is the maximum $3 per $1,000, making the total transfer tax $4 per $1,000 of the sale price. On a $350,000 home, that is $1,400.

In Delaware County (Delaware, Powell, parts of Westerville), the permissive tax is also $3 per $1,000, for the same $4 per $1,000 total rate.

The conveyance fee is paid by the seller in Ohio. It is calculated on the full sale price, with no exemptions for the first portion of value like some other states offer. This fee is not negotiable; it is set by law.

Title Insurance (Owner's Policy)

In Central Ohio, it is customary for the seller to pay for the owner's title insurance policy, which protects the buyer against any title defects, liens, or ownership disputes that may exist on the property. The cost is based on the sale price and typically runs around 0.5% of the sale price, though the exact premium depends on the title company and the complexity of the title search.

On a $350,000 home, expect to pay roughly $1,500 to $1,800 for the owner's title policy. Note that the buyer pays separately for their lender's title insurance policy (required by their mortgage company), so you are only responsible for the owner's policy.

While the seller customarily pays this cost in Central Ohio, it is technically a negotiable item. In a strong seller's market, you may be able to negotiate for the buyer to cover this expense.

Prorated Property Taxes

Ohio property taxes are paid in arrears, meaning you pay for the previous year's taxes in the current year. At closing, your property taxes are prorated so that you pay your share through the date of closing and the buyer assumes responsibility from that point forward.

The amount depends on your home's tax valuation, the local millage rate, and the time of year you close. Franklin County property tax rates vary by school district and municipality, but a common effective rate for a Columbus home is roughly 1.8% to 2.5% of the auditor's appraised value annually. If you close midway through the tax year, you would owe approximately half of the annual tax bill as a prorated amount.

For a $350,000 home with an annual property tax bill around $6,000, closing in June would mean prorated taxes of roughly $3,000 on the settlement statement. The exact amount is calculated to the day by the title company.

Attorney and Closing Fees

In Ohio, real estate transactions can be closed by either a title company or a real estate attorney. The closing fee, sometimes called the settlement fee, typically ranges from $500 to $1,000 for the seller's side. This covers the preparation of the deed, settlement statement, and coordination of the closing process.

Some title companies charge a flat fee; others charge separately for title search, document preparation, and closing services. It is worth asking your agent to recommend title companies that offer competitive pricing without cutting corners on service quality.

Home Warranty

A home warranty is an optional expense that some sellers offer to buyers as an incentive. It covers the repair or replacement of major home systems (HVAC, plumbing, electrical) and appliances for typically one year after closing. The cost is usually $400 to $600 for a basic plan.

In a competitive market, you probably do not need to offer a home warranty. In a slower market or if your home has older systems, offering a warranty can make your listing more attractive and give buyers confidence. Your listing agent can advise on whether this makes sense for your specific situation.

Repair Credits from Inspection

After a buyer's home inspection, it is common for the buyer to request repairs or credits for issues found during the inspection. The amount is entirely negotiable and can range from nothing to tens of thousands of dollars depending on the property's condition.

This is one area where a pre-listing inspection provides enormous value. By identifying issues before you list, you can address them on your terms, price your home accordingly, or disclose them upfront. This reduces the chance of a buyer using their inspection as leverage to renegotiate a lower price.

Recording Fees

The county recorder charges a small fee to record the new deed transferring ownership to the buyer. In Franklin County, recording fees typically run $50 to $150 depending on the number of pages in the deed and any additional documents that need to be recorded. This is a minor cost in the grand scheme of the transaction.

How to Reduce Your Closing Costs

While some closing costs are fixed by law (like the transfer tax), there are several strategies to reduce what you pay overall.

1. Work with a Low-Commission Listing Agent

This is the single biggest lever you have. The difference between a 3% listing commission and a 1% commission on a $350,000 home is $7,000. At TD Realty Ohio, you get full-service representation including MLS listing, professional photography, showing coordination, offer review, counteroffer drafting, and negotiation support, and a free pre-listing home inspection for just 1-2% instead of 3%.

2. Get a Pre-Listing Inspection

Repair credits negotiated after a buyer's inspection are one of the most unpredictable closing costs. By getting a pre-listing inspection before you list, you can identify and address issues proactively. This often reduces or eliminates repair credits at the negotiating table, because buyers have fewer surprises to use as leverage.

3. Price Your Home Accurately from the Start

Overpricing leads to longer time on market, which leads to price reductions and weaker negotiating positions. When buyers sense desperation, they ask for more concessions. A well-priced home that generates strong interest from the beginning puts you in control of the negotiation. Get a free home value estimate to start with accurate pricing data.

4. Negotiate the Title Insurance

While the seller customarily pays for the owner's title policy in Central Ohio, this is a negotiable item. In a strong seller's market or with a highly motivated buyer, you may be able to shift this cost to the buyer's side of the settlement statement. Your listing agent can advise on whether this is realistic in your current market conditions.

5. Time Your Closing Strategically

Since property taxes are prorated, the timing of your closing affects how much you owe. If you close shortly after a tax payment has been made, your prorated amount will be smaller. This is a minor optimization, but on a high-tax property, it can save a few hundred dollars.

6. Skip the Home Warranty Unless Necessary

In a competitive market where homes are selling quickly, you likely do not need to offer a home warranty as a buyer incentive. Save that $400-$600 unless your agent advises otherwise based on market conditions for your specific property.

Franklin County vs. Delaware County: Key Differences

Many Central Ohio sellers, especially those in the Westerville, Powell, and Lewis Center areas, need to understand the differences between Franklin County and Delaware County, since these communities straddle the county line.

Transfer Tax

Both Franklin County and Delaware County charge the maximum permissive rate of $3 per $1,000 on top of the $1 per $1,000 state fee, resulting in the same $4 per $1,000 total transfer tax. So for transfer tax purposes, there is no difference between the two counties.

Property Taxes

This is where the difference is significant. Delaware County property tax rates are generally lower than Franklin County for comparable home values, thanks to different school district levies and municipal tax structures. However, Delaware County has seen rapid growth and increasing property valuations, so the actual dollar amount you pay depends on your specific location and the latest triennial reappraisal.

For prorated property tax purposes at closing, a home in Delaware County may have a lower prorated tax amount than a similarly priced home in Franklin County, which means slightly lower closing costs on that specific line item.

Title and Closing Practices

Title insurance rates and closing practices are generally consistent across both counties, as most title companies operate throughout Central Ohio. The same title company can typically handle closings in either county. You should not see meaningful differences in title fees, attorney costs, or recording fees between Franklin and Delaware counties.

Market Conditions

Delaware County, particularly Powell and Olentangy school district areas, tends to have higher median home prices than much of Franklin County. Higher sale prices mean proportionally higher closing costs on percentage-based items like title insurance and commissions. However, the commission savings from using a low-commission agent also scale up, making the benefit of working with TD Realty Ohio even more significant on higher-priced Delaware County homes.

Understanding Your Closing Disclosure

Before closing, you will receive a settlement statement (sometimes called a closing disclosure on the seller side, or an ALTA statement) that itemizes every charge. Review this document carefully, ideally a few days before closing, to verify that all charges match what you expected. Look for:

  • Commission rates match your listing agreement
  • Transfer tax is calculated correctly on the actual sale price
  • Prorated taxes are based on the correct annual amount and prorated to the closing date
  • Title fees match the title company's quote
  • Repair credits reflect what was agreed upon in the inspection resolution
  • Mortgage payoff (if applicable) is accurate, including per diem interest through the closing date

If anything looks incorrect, raise it with your listing agent immediately. Errors on settlement statements happen, and they are much easier to correct before closing than after.
