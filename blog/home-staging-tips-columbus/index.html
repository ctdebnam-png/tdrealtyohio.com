Home Staging Tips That Help Columbus Homes Sell Faster

TD Realty Ohio | February 2026

If you're preparing to sell your Columbus home, staging is one of the most effective tools you have to maximize your sale price and minimize your time on the market. But staging doesn't mean turning your house into a magazine cover. It means making strategic, intentional choices that help buyers see themselves living in your home.

After helping sellers across Central Ohio list and sell their homes, TD Realty Ohio has seen the difference that staging makes firsthand. Homes that are thoughtfully staged consistently attract more showings, generate stronger offers, and close faster than comparable homes that aren't staged. This guide covers the specific staging strategies that work in the Columbus market, room by room, so you can decide exactly where to invest your time and money.

Why Home Staging Matters: What the Data Shows

Staging isn't just about aesthetics. It's a proven sales strategy backed by real numbers. According to the National Association of REALTORS' 2024 Profile of Home Staging, 81% of buyer's agents said staging made it easier for their clients to visualize a property as a future home. That visualization is what drives offers.

Here's what the data tells us about staged vs. unstaged homes:

  • Higher sale prices: Staged homes sell for 1-5% more on average. On a $300,000 Columbus home, that's $3,000 to $15,000 in additional proceeds.
  • Faster sales: Staged homes spend 33-50% less time on the market. In a market where every week on the MLS costs you carrying expenses, that difference matters.
  • Better online presence: Over 95% of home searches start online. Staged homes photograph better, which means more clicks, more showings, and more competition among buyers.
  • Stronger first impressions: Buyers form an opinion about a home within the first 7-10 seconds of walking through the door. Staging ensures that first impression works in your favor.

In the Columbus market, where inventory levels fluctuate seasonally and buyers are increasingly price-conscious, every advantage counts. Staging is one of the few seller investments that consistently returns more than it costs.

The Rooms That Matter Most

Not every room carries equal weight with buyers. If you're working with a limited budget or timeline, focus your staging efforts on these four areas in order of impact:

1. The Living Room

The living room is where buyers emotionally connect with your home. It's usually the first room they see after entering, and it sets the tone for the entire showing. A well-staged living room feels spacious, inviting, and comfortable. A cluttered or poorly arranged one makes the whole house feel smaller and less appealing.

The goal is to create a clear conversation area that demonstrates the room's purpose and scale. Buyers need to mentally place their own furniture, so you're showing them the possibility of the space, not your personal decorating style.

2. The Kitchen

Kitchens sell houses. Every real estate agent knows this, and the data backs it up. Buyers spend more mental energy evaluating the kitchen than any other room because kitchen renovations are expensive. They're calculating: can this kitchen work as-is, or will a $30,000 update be necessary?

Your job when staging the kitchen isn't to renovate. It's to present the kitchen in its absolute best light so buyers focus on its strengths rather than its weaknesses. Clear countertops, clean appliances, and organized cabinets go further than you might expect.

3. The Master Bedroom

Buyers want the master bedroom to feel like a retreat. It should feel calm, spacious, and restful. This is the room where buyers imagine ending their day, so the emotional appeal matters more than square footage. A thoughtfully staged master bedroom can make a modest room feel luxurious.

4. The Bathrooms

Bathrooms are the cleanliness litmus test. If a bathroom feels dirty or dated, buyers assume the rest of the house has hidden maintenance issues. A sparkling clean bathroom with fresh towels and minimal clutter signals that the home has been well cared for. Fortunately, bathrooms are also the easiest and cheapest rooms to stage effectively.

Room-by-Room Staging Checklist

Here are specific, actionable steps for staging each key area of your Columbus home:

Living Room Staging Checklist

  • Declutter ruthlessly. Remove at least 50% of decorative items, books, and accessories. Less is more. Pack it now; you're moving anyway.
  • Remove personal photos. Family photos make it your home, not the buyer's future home. Replace them with neutral artwork or remove entirely.
  • Arrange furniture for conversation. Pull furniture away from walls to create a defined seating area. This actually makes rooms feel larger.
  • Remove excess furniture. If the room has a couch, loveseat, recliner, and two side tables, consider removing the recliner. Open floor space reads as square footage.
  • Add a few strategic accessories. A throw blanket on the couch, a simple centerpiece on the coffee table, and one or two plants create warmth without clutter.
  • Clean windows and open blinds. Natural light makes rooms feel bigger and more inviting. Central Ohio winter light is limited, so maximize every bit you can get.
  • Address the lighting. Replace any burnt-out bulbs. If the room lacks overhead lighting (common in older Columbus homes), add floor or table lamps to eliminate dark corners.

Kitchen Staging Checklist

  • Clear the countertops. Remove everything except one or two decorative items (a cutting board, a small plant, or a fruit bowl). Countertop space is a top buyer priority.
  • Clean appliances inside and out. Buyers open ovens, microwaves, and dishwashers. Make sure they're spotless.
  • Organize cabinets and pantry. Buyers will look inside. Neat, organized cabinets suggest ample storage. Half-empty is better than overstuffed.
  • Remove magnets and papers from the refrigerator. A bare fridge front looks cleaner and makes the kitchen feel more polished.
  • Add fresh hand towels. Swap out worn kitchen towels for new, neutral-colored ones.
  • Fix minor issues. Tighten loose cabinet handles, fix dripping faucets, replace cracked switch plates. These five-minute fixes eliminate red flags.
  • Consider a fresh coat of paint. If your kitchen walls are a bold or dated color, repainting in a warm neutral (Agreeable Gray, Accessible Beige, or White Dove are reliable choices) can transform the feel for under $100.

Master Bedroom Staging Checklist

  • Make the bed the focal point. Invest in a new white or neutral comforter set if yours is worn or boldly patterned. Add two to four pillows for a hotel-like feel.
  • Remove all furniture except the bed and nightstands. That exercise bike, the desk in the corner, the extra bookshelf? They're making the room feel smaller. Move them to the garage temporarily.
  • Clear the closets. Organize and remove at least 30% of your clothing. Buyers open closets, and a half-full closet looks twice as big as a packed one.
  • Add matching nightstand lamps. Symmetry in the bedroom creates a sense of order and calm.
  • Remove TVs if possible. A bedroom without a TV feels more like a restful retreat.

Bathroom Staging Checklist

  • Deep clean everything. Scrub grout, clean around the base of the toilet, remove any mildew, and make fixtures shine. This is non-negotiable.
  • Replace old caulk. Yellowed or cracked caulk around the tub and sink is a $10 fix that makes the bathroom look years newer.
  • Put out fresh white towels. White towels read as clean and spa-like. Roll them or fold them neatly on open shelving or a towel bar.
  • Remove all personal products. Clear the shower of bottles. Put away toothbrushes, razors, and medications. A few staged items (a soap dispenser, a small plant, a candle) are fine.
  • Add a new shower curtain. If you have a shower curtain, replace it with a clean, simple white one. This costs $10-$15 and makes a noticeable difference.
  • Check the mirror and lighting. Clean the mirror until it's spotless. Replace dim bulbs with brighter ones. Good bathroom lighting flatters both the room and the buyer looking in the mirror.

Curb Appeal and Exterior Staging

Buyers form their first impression before they walk through the front door. In Columbus and Central Ohio, exterior staging requires thinking about seasonal considerations that sellers in warmer climates don't face.

Year-Round Curb Appeal Essentials

  • Power wash the driveway, walkways, and siding. Ohio weather deposits grime and mildew on exterior surfaces. A $200 power washing can make a house look freshly painted.
  • Paint or replace the front door. The front door is the visual anchor of your home's exterior. A freshly painted door in a complementary color (navy, black, or dark green work well with Columbus's many Colonial and Cape Cod styles) makes the entire facade look polished.
  • Update house numbers and the mailbox. Outdated or hard-to-read house numbers signal neglect. Modern, visible numbers cost $15-$30 and take 10 minutes to install.
  • Clean gutters and downspouts. Clogged gutters with debris hanging over the edge are an immediate red flag for buyers worried about water damage, a common concern with Ohio basements.
  • Ensure outdoor lighting works. Many Columbus showings happen in late afternoon during fall and winter when daylight is limited. Working porch lights and pathway lighting matter more here than in Sun Belt markets.

Spring and Summer Staging (March - September)

  • Mow the lawn weekly and edge along sidewalks and beds.
  • Add fresh mulch to landscape beds. Brown or black hardwood mulch is the Central Ohio standard and costs $3-$5 per bag.
  • Plant seasonal color near the entrance: petunias, impatiens, or mums depending on the month.
  • Trim overgrown shrubs and hedges, especially anything blocking windows or the front entry.
  • Set up a simple porch seating area if space allows (two chairs and a small table suggest usable outdoor living space).

Fall and Winter Staging (October - February)

  • Keep leaves raked and off walkways. Wet leaves on sidewalks are a safety hazard and look neglected.
  • Add a seasonal wreath to the front door (keep it simple and non-holiday-specific if possible).
  • Place weather-appropriate doormats at entries. A clean, quality doormat signals care.
  • Keep snow and ice cleared from walkways and the driveway promptly. Buyers who slip on your sidewalk are not writing strong offers.
  • Add planters with winter-hardy greenery like boxwood or holly near the entrance.
  • Make sure the house feels warm when buyers arrive. Set the thermostat to 68-70 degrees for showings. A cold house feels unwelcoming and makes buyers worry about heating costs.

In neighborhoods like Westerville and Upper Arlington, where buyers expect well-maintained properties, curb appeal can make or break a first showing. Don't underestimate the power of a tidy exterior.

DIY Staging vs. Professional Staging

One of the most common questions Columbus sellers ask is whether they should hire a professional stager or handle it themselves. The answer depends on your budget, your home's condition, and your own willingness to do the work.

DIY Staging: $0 - $500

Best for: Owner-occupied homes in good condition that primarily need decluttering, cleaning, and minor touch-ups.

Most of the staging checklist above can be done yourself at little to no cost. The biggest expense in DIY staging is typically paint ($30-$50 per room), new towels and bedding ($100-$200), and small accessories like plants or candles ($50-$100).

Pros:

  • Minimal cost, maximum ROI
  • You control the timeline
  • Works well for homes that are already in reasonably good shape
  • Most sellers can accomplish 80% of what a professional stager would recommend

Cons:

  • Requires honest self-assessment (can you really see your home through a buyer's eyes?)
  • Time-consuming, especially if you're also managing a job and family
  • You're limited to your existing furniture and accessories

Professional Staging: $1,500 - $3,000

Best for: Vacant homes, higher-end properties ($400,000+), homes with dated decor or unusual layouts, and sellers who want maximum impact.

Professional stagers in Columbus typically charge $300-$500 for an initial consultation and staging plan. Full staging (where they bring in rented furniture, artwork, and accessories) runs $1,500-$3,000 for the first month, depending on the home's size and the number of rooms staged. Additional months are typically $500-$800.

Pros:

  • Expert eye for layout, color, and buyer psychology
  • Access to furniture and accessories you don't own
  • Essential for vacant homes (empty rooms photograph poorly and feel smaller in person)
  • Can transform dated spaces without renovation

Cons:

  • Higher upfront cost
  • Monthly rental fees add up if the home takes longer to sell
  • Scheduling and coordination required

When Professional Staging Pays for Itself

Based on TD Realty Ohio's experience working with Columbus sellers, professional staging makes the most financial sense in three situations:

  1. Vacant homes. Empty rooms are hard for buyers to visualize. Photos of empty rooms perform poorly on Zillow and Realtor.com. Furnished staged photos get significantly more clicks and showing requests.
  2. Homes priced above $400,000. At higher price points, the 1-5% staging premium represents a substantial dollar amount. On a $500,000 home, a 2% increase from staging is $10,000, far exceeding the $2,000-$3,000 staging cost.
  3. Homes that have been sitting on the market. If your home has been listed for 30+ days without offers, professional staging combined with new photography can essentially relaunch the listing.

Common Staging Mistakes That Hurt Your Sale

These mistakes appear repeatedly in Central Ohio home listings. Avoiding them is just as important as following the staging checklists above.

Mistake 1: Over-Personalizing

Your home reflects your personality, and that's wonderful for living. But for selling, personal items create a barrier. Family photos, religious items, political signs, sports memorabilia, and collections (no matter how impressive) make it harder for buyers to imagine the home as theirs. Pack these items early.

Mistake 2: Ignoring Odors

You've gone nose-blind to your home's smell. Pet odors, cooking smells, cigarette smoke, and musty basements are deal-breakers for buyers. Before listing, have a friend with a honest personality walk through and tell you what they smell. Address the source (deep clean carpets, wash walls, clean the HVAC ducts), don't just mask it with air fresheners. Buyers notice when a home is heavily perfumed and assume you're hiding something.

Mistake 3: Leaving Clutter in "Hidden" Spaces

Buyers open closets, cabinets, the garage, and basement storage areas. A beautifully staged living room loses its impact when the buyer opens the hall closet and an avalanche of shoes falls out. Stage your storage spaces too. Rent a storage unit if needed; it typically costs $50-$100 per month in the Columbus area.

Mistake 4: Dark Rooms

Ohio's overcast skies mean natural light is already at a premium, especially November through March. Heavy curtains, dim bulbs, and closed blinds make rooms feel small and gloomy. Open every blind, turn on every light, and upgrade to brighter bulbs (soft white, 2700K-3000K, in 60-75 watt equivalent). The difference is dramatic.

Mistake 5: Staging Around Rather Than Fixing Problems

Strategically placing a rug over a stained carpet or a bookshelf in front of a wall crack doesn't fool anyone. Buyers will move the rug, look behind the bookshelf, and now they don't trust you. Address problems directly or disclose them openly. Trying to hide issues always backfires.

Mistake 6: Over-Staging

There's a line between staged and artificial. Homes that look like furniture showrooms or hotel lobbies can feel sterile and unwelcoming. The goal is "someone with great taste lives here," not "no one actually lives here." A lived-in feel, achieved through a few personal touches like a book on a nightstand or a coffee mug on the counter, keeps the home feeling warm and realistic.

How Staging Works with the Pre-Listing Inspection Process

At TD Realty Ohio, we include a free pre-listing inspection with every listing. This inspection and your staging efforts should work together in a deliberate sequence.

Here's the recommended process:

  1. Get the pre-listing inspection first. Before you spend time and money staging, know what condition your home is in. The inspection may reveal issues that need repair before staging makes sense. For example, there's no point in staging a bathroom if the inspection reveals plumbing issues you'll need to fix.
  2. Make strategic repairs. Based on the inspection results, address the issues that will most impact buyer perception and your sale price. (Read more about how pre-listing inspections benefit sellers.)
  3. Stage after repairs are complete. Now that your home is in its best structural and mechanical condition, staging puts the finishing touches on the presentation.
  4. Schedule professional photography. Once staging is complete, professional photos capture your home at its best for the MLS listing.

This sequence, inspection then repairs then staging then photography, produces the best results because each step builds on the previous one. Staging a home with known issues is like putting lipstick on a pig. Fixing issues but not staging leaves money on the table. Doing both in the right order maximizes your net proceeds.

The Role of Professional Photography in Selling Staged Homes

Staging your home and then photographing it with your phone is like buying a tailored suit and wearing it to a Zoom call with the camera off. The whole point of staging is to create a visual presentation that attracts buyers, and that presentation lives or dies in the listing photos.

Professional real estate photography matters because:

  • Online first impressions are everything. The vast majority of buyers find homes online before scheduling a showing. Your listing photos are your home's first showing, and you don't get a second chance at a first impression.
  • Wide-angle lenses show rooms accurately. Professional photographers use wide-angle lenses that capture the full scope of a room without distortion. Phone photos make rooms look smaller than they are.
  • Proper lighting makes staged rooms shine. Photographers use flash blending and HDR techniques to balance interior and exterior light, making rooms look bright, airy, and inviting regardless of Ohio's weather that day.
  • Composition tells a story. A professional photographer knows which angles highlight a room's best features and draw the eye where you want it to go.

Listings with professional photos receive significantly more online views and showing requests than those with amateur photos. At TD Realty Ohio, professional photography is part of our listing service because we know it directly impacts your bottom line.

Putting It All Together: Your Staging Action Plan

Here's a practical timeline for staging your Columbus home before listing:

4-6 Weeks Before Listing:

  • Complete the pre-listing inspection
  • Begin decluttering room by room (plan to fill at least 10-15 boxes)
  • Rent a storage unit or arrange to store boxes at a friend or family member's home
  • Get quotes for any repairs identified in the inspection

2-4 Weeks Before Listing:

  • Complete repairs
  • Paint any rooms that need refreshing
  • Deep clean the entire house (consider hiring a professional cleaning service for $200-$400)
  • Address any exterior curb appeal items

1 Week Before Listing:

  • Complete final staging touches (new towels, bedding, accessories)
  • Arrange furniture per your staging plan
  • If using a professional stager, this is when they set up

2-3 Days Before Listing:

  • Professional photography session
  • Final walk-through to ensure everything is in place
  • Set up a showing routine (quick checklist for keeping the home "show ready" daily)
