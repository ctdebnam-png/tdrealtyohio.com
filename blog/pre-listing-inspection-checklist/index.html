Pre-Listing Inspection Checklist for Central Ohio Sellers

By Travis Debnam | February 2026 | 8 min read

A pre-listing inspection gives you a clear picture of your home's condition before buyers get involved. Here is what inspectors check and what you can do to prepare for each area of the home.

Why Prepare for a Pre-Listing Inspection

A pre-listing inspection identifies issues before they become negotiation problems. When selling your home, knowing the condition upfront lets you decide which repairs to make, how to price accurately, and what to disclose. It removes surprises from the transaction.

TD Realty Ohio includes a free pre-listing inspection with every listing. The checklist below will help you prepare so the process goes smoothly.

Exterior: Roof, Gutters, and Siding

What inspectors check: Roof covering condition, missing or damaged shingles, flashing around chimneys and vents, gutter attachment and drainage direction, siding condition, and fascia or soffit damage.

How to prepare: Clear debris from gutters and downspouts. Walk the perimeter and note any visible damage to siding or trim. If you can safely view the roof from ground level, look for missing shingles or sagging areas. Make sure downspouts direct water away from the foundation.

Foundation and Grading

What inspectors check: Visible cracks in foundation walls, grading slope around the home, signs of water intrusion, and condition of exterior concrete (driveways, walkways, stoops).

How to prepare: Ensure the ground slopes away from the foundation on all sides. Fill any settled areas near the foundation with soil. Note any cracks wider than a quarter inch for discussion with the inspector. In Westerville and Columbus, older homes may have stone or block foundations that are more prone to moisture — make sure the area around the foundation drains well.

HVAC System

What inspectors check: Age and condition of the furnace and air conditioner, filter condition, thermostat operation, visible ductwork condition, and whether the system heats and cools as expected.

How to prepare: Replace the air filter. Make sure all supply and return vents are unobstructed. Have the system serviced if it has not been maintained recently. Keep any maintenance records available for the inspector. Know the age of your furnace and AC unit — inspectors will note this in the report.

Plumbing

What inspectors check: Faucet and fixture operation, visible pipe condition, water pressure, drain speed, toilet function, and signs of leaks under sinks and around water heater connections.

How to prepare: Run every faucet and flush every toilet. Check under all sinks for drips or moisture. Make sure the main water shut-off valve is accessible and labeled. If you have a slow drain, address it before the inspection.

Water Heater

What inspectors check: Age and capacity, temperature and pressure relief valve, proper venting (for gas units), visible corrosion, and whether the unit is producing hot water at the expected temperature.

How to prepare: Check the data plate for the manufacture date. Make sure the area around the water heater is clear with at least a few feet of access. Verify the TPR (temperature/pressure relief) valve has a discharge pipe that terminates within six inches of the floor.

Electrical System

What inspectors check: Panel condition and labeling, breaker operation, GFCI protection in wet areas (kitchens, bathrooms, garages, exteriors), outlet and switch function, and visible wiring condition.

How to prepare: Test all GFCI outlets using the test/reset buttons. Make sure the electrical panel is accessible (no storage blocking it) and that breakers are labeled. Replace any burned-out light bulbs so the inspector can verify fixtures work. If you have an older home in Dublin or Powell with a Federal Pacific or Zinsco panel, be aware that these are commonly flagged.

Windows and Doors

What inspectors check: Operation of all windows and exterior doors, glass condition (fogged double-pane units indicate seal failure), weatherstripping, locks, and screen condition.

How to prepare: Open and close every window and exterior door. Look for fogged or cloudy glass in double-pane windows. Make sure all locks engage properly. Replace any missing or torn screens.

Basement and Crawlspace

What inspectors check: Signs of moisture or water intrusion, structural elements (joists, beams, posts), sump pump operation, insulation condition, and any visible mold or mildew.

How to prepare: Clear stored items away from walls so the inspector can access all areas. If you have a sump pump, pour water into the pit to verify it activates. Check for efflorescence (white mineral deposits) on basement walls, which indicates past moisture. Run a dehumidifier if the space is damp.

Garage

What inspectors check: Garage door opener operation and auto-reverse safety, fire separation between garage and living space, floor condition, and GFCI protection for outlets.

How to prepare: Test the garage door auto-reverse by placing an object in the door's path. Make sure the door between the garage and living space closes and latches fully. Verify the garage has a GFCI-protected outlet.

Smoke and CO Detectors

What inspectors check: Presence and placement of smoke detectors (required on each level and near bedrooms), carbon monoxide detectors (required near sleeping areas in Ohio if the home has fuel-burning appliances or an attached garage), and whether units are functional.

How to prepare: Test all smoke and CO detectors. Replace batteries or entire units if they are older than 10 years (smoke) or 7 years (CO). Install any missing detectors before the inspection.

Ohio-Specific Items

Radon Testing

Central Ohio has areas with elevated radon levels. While not legally required, radon testing is a common part of the home sale process. Testing proactively gives you time to install a mitigation system if levels are above the EPA action level of 4 pCi/L. A mitigation system typically costs between $800 and $1,500.

Well and Septic (If Applicable)

Homes outside municipal water and sewer systems need well water testing and septic inspections. Well tests check for bacteria and nitrates. Septic inspections assess tank condition, drain field function, and whether the system has been pumped on schedule. If your home in outlying Central Ohio areas uses well or septic, plan for these tests before listing.

After the Inspection

Once you have the inspection report, review it with your agent to decide which items to address. Focus on safety concerns, code issues, and anything that could affect financing or appraisal. Cosmetic items are generally less important. A well-prepared pre-listing inspection report builds buyer confidence and can reduce the back-and-forth during negotiations.
