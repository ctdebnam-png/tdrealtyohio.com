Commercial guide

Selling a gas station or former fuel site in central Ohio

Fuel sites sell. They take more preparation than a strip center, because every buyer and every lender asks the same questions about the tanks. Here's what they ask, and how we get ahead of it before your property goes on the market.

What buyers and lenders ask for

  • Tank records. Registration and inspection history for every tank on the site, from the Ohio State Fire Marshal's Bureau of Underground Storage Tank Regulations (BUSTR).
  • Closure paperwork. If tanks were pulled, the closure report and the state's letter accepting it.
  • Release history. Whether a release was ever reported, and if so, whether it has a No Further Action letter or is still open.
  • Contracts. Fuel supply and branding agreements, which may transfer to a buyer, carry a buyout, or end at sale.
  • Equipment and permits. The age of the tanks, lines, dispensers and canopy, and the local permits that go with them.
  • The lender's own report. Almost every buyer's lender orders its own environmental report on a fuel site. We plan time for it in the contract so it doesn't sink the closing date.

What to pull together before you list

Records we'll help you request

  • The BUSTR facility file for your site.
  • Any closure or No Further Action letters.
  • Tank and line tightness tests from recent years.
  • Your coverage history with Ohio's Petroleum UST Release Compensation Board.

Business records, for an operating sale

  • Fuel gallons by month for the last two to three years.
  • Inside sales, if the store sells with the land.
  • Current fuel supply or branding agreement.
  • Leases on any part of the site.

How long it usually takes

  • Two to four weeks to gather records and set a price.
  • Two to four months on the market, depending on location and whether it sells as a fuel site or for redevelopment.
  • Six to twelve weeks from contract to closing. The buyer's lender review on a fuel site runs longer than on most commercial property, and we build that into the timeline from the start.

What drives the price

  • Whether the site is operating or closed, and whether any release is closed or still open.
  • Fuel volume and inside sales, for a buyer who will keep running it.
  • Corner position, traffic counts, curb cuts and access.
  • Value for another use, such as quick-service food, a car wash or retail, when the land is worth more than the business.
  • Any deed restrictions left from a past cleanup.
